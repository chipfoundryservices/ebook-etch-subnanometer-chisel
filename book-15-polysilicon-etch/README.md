## Overview

*Polysilicon Plasma Etch & Gate Definition* explores the specialized plasma etch processes required to pattern polysilicon gates—a critical step in semiconductor manufacturing that directly determines:

- **Gate length precision** (±5nm variation impacts device performance)
- **Sidewall profile** (straight vs. sloped affects capacitance coupling)
- **Selectivity to underlying layers** (prevents damage to gate oxide)
- **Equipment capital intensity** ($8-12M per chamber, highest cost in fab)

This book covers:

- **Polysilicon etch chemistry** (HCl vs. Cl₂ plasma, chemistry trade-offs)
- **Gate selectivity engineering** (stopping on oxide without damage)
- **Pattern transfer fidelity** (CD uniformity, corner rounding, line roughness)
- **Etch equipment specialization** (dedicated poly chambers vs. multi-purpose)
- **Capital allocation** (poly etch capex, maintenance, technology transitions)

## Strategic Importance

Polysilicon etch is one of the **highest-complexity process steps** in semiconductor manufacturing:

1. **Extreme selectivity requirement:** Stop precisely on 5-10nm gate oxide
2. **Damage sensitivity:** Excess ion energy damages oxide → device failure
3. **Pattern fidelity:** Must preserve CD precision through dense interconnect
4. **High capex:** Dedicated chambers cost $10-15M (vs. $8-10M generic etch)

**Economic significance:** Gate etch chambers are typically 10-15% of fab capex, but their contribution to yield and performance determines whether fab succeeds or fails.
