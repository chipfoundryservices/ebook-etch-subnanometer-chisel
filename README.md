**Book #4 in the ChipFoundryServices Technical Series**

## Overview

*Etch: The Sub-Nanometer Chisel* is a comprehensive exploration of plasma-based reactive ion etching (RIE), atomic layer etch (ALE), and the physics of sub-nanometer semiconductor device fabrication. This book bridges fundamental plasma physics with industrial equipment engineering and economic moat analysis, revealing why companies like **Lam Research** and **Applied Materials** command durable competitive advantages worth tens of billions of dollars.

Following the Charlie Munger lattice-work model of thinking, each chapter combines:
- **Physical first principles** (plasma dynamics, ion sheath physics, radical chemistry)
- **Industrial process secrets** (chamber design, selectivity mechanisms, defect avoidance)
- **Economic moats** (equipment capex cycles, switching costs, gross margin durability)
- **Capital allocation lessons** (equipment depreciation, service revenue annuities, technology transitions)

## Key Themes

### 1. **The Inversion Principle**
Understanding etch starts with catastrophic failures: charge damage, aspect-ratio-dependent microloading, bowing, undercut. We work backward from what *must not happen* to design chambers that succeed at atomic scale.

### 2. **The Economics of Equipment Monopoly**
Why can Lam Research sustain 45%+ gross margins? Answer: switching costs, installed-base lock-in, yield learning curves, and the $10M+ price per chamber create an unbreakable moat.

### 3. **The Plasma Frontier**
Electrons at 20,000 K collide with molecules at 300 K. This violent asymmetry is the source of all etch power. Mastering it means controlling silicon at sub-angstrom precision.

### 4. **The Moore's Law Dependency**
Every new node shrink (7nm → 5nm → 3nm) requires new etch capabilities. Equipment suppliers who lead this innovation capture years of revenue advantage before competitors reverse-engineer.
