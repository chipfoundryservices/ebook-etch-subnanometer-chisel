# Glossary: Etch Terminology and Physics

---

## A

**ALE** — Atomic Layer Etch. A cyclic, self-limiting etch process achieving angstrom-scale precision. Alternates between selective adsorption and ion-assisted desorption.

**Anisotropic etch** — Etch that occurs primarily in one direction (vertical), with minimal lateral etch. Opposite of isotropic.

**Anisotropy ratio** — The ratio of vertical etch rate to lateral (horizontal) etch rate. Values > 10:1 are considered highly anisotropic.

**ARDE** — Aspect-Ratio-Dependent Etch. The phenomenon where features with different aspect ratios etch at different rates. A major challenge in advanced etching.

**Aspect ratio** — The ratio of depth to width of an etched feature. A 100nm-deep trench 10nm wide has aspect ratio 10:1.

**Ar⁺** — Argon ion. A commonly used inert ion in plasma etch, particularly for sputtering.

---

## B

**Bias electrode** — The electrode (typically at the wafer) that is biased (held at DC voltage) to accelerate ions toward the substrate.

**Bias voltage** (or **Self-bias voltage**) — The DC voltage that develops between plasma and electrode surfaces, typically -100V to -3000V. Accelerates ions.

**Breakdown** (electrical) — The point at which an electric field becomes strong enough to cause ionization in a gas, initiating plasma formation.

---

## C

**CCP** — Capacitively Coupled Plasma. An RF plasma where the electrode is capacitively coupled to the plasma (via displacement current). Power and ion energy are coupled.

**Charge damage** — Degradation of gate dielectric caused by injection of energetic ions or electrons during processing. Major concern at sub-5nm nodes.

**Chemically enhanced sputtering** — Etch mechanism combining chemical radical attack and ion bombardment for increased etch rate.

**Cl₂** — Chlorine gas. Used in etch chemistry for metal and polysilicon etching.

**CMP** — Chemical-Mechanical Polishing. Post-etch planarization step to remove excess material.

**Cold plasma** — A plasma where ions are at relatively low temperature (< 10,000 K) while electrons are hot. Standard in semiconductor etch.

**Coulomb force** — The attractive/repulsive force between charged particles. Basis for electrostatic chuck operation.

---

## D

**DC bias** — Direct current (DC) voltage applied to an electrode. In etch, creates the voltage that accelerates ions.

**Debye length** — The characteristic length over which electric fields are shielded by plasma ions and electrons. Typically 0.1–1 mm in etch chambers.

**Deposition** — Build-up of material (typically polymer) on chamber walls or wafer surfaces during etch.

**Desorption** — Removal of adsorbed atoms/molecules from a surface by ion bombardment or thermal energy.

**Dielectric** — An insulating material (e.g., SiO₂, Si₃N₄, HfO₂). Primary substrate for dielectric etch.

**Dissociation** — Breaking of a chemical bond. When plasma electrons dissociate CF₄, it breaks into CF₃, CF₂, CF, and F radicals.

**Dual-frequency bias** — Application of two different RF frequencies (e.g., 13.56 MHz and 400 kHz) to bias electrode simultaneously. Enables independent control of average ion energy and energy distribution.

---

## E

**Etch rate** — Speed at which material is removed, typically measured in nm/min or nm/sec.

**Etch selectivity** — See *Selectivity*.

**Etch uniformity** — The degree to which etch rate is consistent across the wafer. Typically expressed as ±% variation.

**ESC** — Electrostatic Chuck. Wafer holding device using Coulomb force instead of mechanical clamps.

**Excimer laser** — High-power UV laser used in lithography. Produces wavelengths of 193 nm (ArF) or 248 nm (KrF).

**Excited state** — An electron orbital configuration with higher energy than ground state. Decays by emitting photons.

---

## F

**F radical** (or $F^•$) — Fluorine radical. A highly reactive species with unpaired electron. Primary etchant in fluorocarbon chemistries.

**FAB** (Fabrication plant) — A semiconductor manufacturing facility. Also called *foundry*.

**Fluorocarbon** — A hydrocarbon molecule containing fluorine atoms (e.g., CF₄, C₄F₈, CHF₃). Primary etch gas in semiconductor processing.

**Fluorine** — Element F (atomic number 9). Most electronegative element; dominant etchant in semiconductor industry.

**Fluorine scavenging** — Reduction in available fluorine radicals due to parasitic reactions. Limits etch rate at high ion densities.

**Free radical** — An atom or molecule with an unpaired electron. Highly reactive. Examples: F•, Cl•, CF₃•.

---

## G

**GAAFET** — Gate-All-Around Field-Effect Transistor. An advanced transistor architecture where gate wraps around all four sides of the channel.

**Gamma (γ)** — In chemistry, a measure of etch selectivity via sputtering yield differences.

**Gas flow** — Rate at which etchant gas is introduced into chamber, typically measured in sccm (standard cubic centimeters per minute).

**Gate dielectric** — Thin insulating layer (SiO₂, HfO₂) between gate electrode and channel. Critical for transistor performance.

---

## H

**H radical** — Hydrogen radical. Can reduce plasma aggressiveness by moderating fluorine reactivity (e.g., in CHF₃).

**HfO₂** — Hafnium dioxide. A high-κ (high dielectric constant) material increasingly used as gate dielectric at advanced nodes.

**High-κ dielectric** — A dielectric with high permittivity (κ > 20). Allows thicker physical layer while maintaining same capacitance, reducing leakage.

---

## I

**ICP** — Inductively Coupled Plasma. RF plasma generated by magnetic field coupling through chamber walls. Decouples plasma generation from ion energy.

**Impedance matching** — Network (L-network, π-network) that matches RF source impedance (50 Ω) to load impedance (plasma + chamber).

**Inductance** — Electrical property of a coil or conductor that stores energy in a magnetic field.

**Ionization** — Process of removing electron from atom/molecule to create ion. Opposite of recombination.

**Ionization potential** — Energy required to remove an electron from an atom. E.g., 12.6 eV for SF₆.

**Ion sheath** — Thin region (mm-scale) near electrode where positive ions accumulate and voltage drop is large. Ions accelerate here.

**Isotropic etch** — Etch that occurs equally in all directions. Results in undercutting. Opposite of anisotropic.

---

## J

**Joule heating** — Heating due to electrical resistance dissipating power: $P = I^2 R$.

---

## K

**Kiyo** — Lam Research's advanced 3D NAND etch chamber with electrostatic focusing ring.

---

## L

**Langmuir probe** — Diagnostic tool consisting of thin wire inserted into plasma. Measures electron temperature and plasma potential.

**Large-scale feature** — In ARDE context, a wide feature (large lateral extent).

**Lattice** — Regular, periodic arrangement of atoms in a crystal.

**Leakage current** — Unwanted electrical current flowing through gate dielectric or junction, typically exponential in electric field.

**Lithography** — Process of creating pattern on wafer using light. Generates mask for subsequent etching.

---

## M

**Macroscopic uniformity** — Etch uniformity across the entire wafer (edge to edge). Typically ±5–10%.

**Mass spectrometry** — Analytical technique measuring mass-to-charge ratios of ions/molecules to identify composition.

**Mean Free Path** — Average distance a particle travels before colliding with another. In plasma, typically mm-scale.

**Microloading** — Variation in etch rate based on local feature density. High-density regions etch slower (radicals depleted).

**Molecular weight** — Mass of one molecule relative to atomic mass unit. CF₄ has MW ≈ 88 amu.

**MTBF** — Mean Time Between Failures. Measure of equipment reliability. Typical etch chamber: 1000–1500 hrs.

---

## N

**Nanosheet** — A thin (3–10 nm) silicon or other semiconductor layer in modern GAA transistors. Can be vertical or horizontal.

**NAND** — Flash memory architecture. Data stored in series of transistors (NAND gates). High density.

**Node** (or **Technology node**) — A generation of semiconductor process (7nm, 5nm, 3nm, etc.). Defining geometry is typically gate length or half-pitch.

---

## O

**OES** — Optical Emission Spectroscopy. Diagnostic measuring light emitted by excited atoms/radicals in plasma.

**Oxide** — Compound containing oxygen. E.g., SiO₂ (silicon dioxide), a common dielectric.

**Oxidation** — Chemical addition of oxygen to a material.

---

## P

**Passivation layer** (or **Polymer passivation**) — Thin carbon-fluorine film deposited on surface during etch. Protects against over-etch.

**Perfluorocarbon** — Hydrocarbon with all hydrogens replaced by fluorine. E.g., CF₄, C₄F₈.

**Phase** (of material) — Solid, liquid, or gas state.

**Plasma** — Ionized gas containing ions, electrons, and radicals. The "fourth state of matter."

**Plasma density** — Number of ions (or electrons) per unit volume. Typically 10¹⁰–10¹² cm⁻³.

**Plasma potential** — Electric potential in bulk plasma. Typically 5–20 V above ground.

**Polymerization** — Formation of polymer (chain of molecules) on surface. Can suppress etch (passivation).

**Pressure** — Measure of gas density. Etch chambers typically 10–500 mTorr.

**Process window** — Range of parameter values (power, pressure, gas flow) within which etch achieves acceptable uniformity and selectivity.

---

## Q

**Quasi-neutrality** — Plasma property where number of ions ≈ number of electrons, ensuring bulk neutrality.

---

## R

**Reactive ion etch** (or **RIE**) — Etch using combination of chemical radical attack and ion bombardment. Standard in semiconductor industry.

**Recombination** — Process where ion captures electron, neutralizing charge. Opposite of ionization.

**Resist** — Photosensitive organic polymer used as mask in lithography. Removed after etch via O₂ plasma or chemical strip.

**RF** — Radiofrequency. In etch, typically 13.56 MHz.

**Rough surface** — Surface with nanometer-scale irregularities. Can degrade device performance.

---

## S

**Sacrificial layer** — Temporary layer removed in a later step. E.g., SiGe under Si nanosheet in GAA device.

**Saturation** — Condition where etch rate reaches maximum and additional power/gas does not increase rate further.

**SCC** — Spontaneous Chemical Corrosion. Corrosion without external voltage/current.

**Sccm** — Standard cubic centimeter per minute. Unit of gas flow rate.

**Selectivity** — Ratio of etch rate for one material vs. another. High selectivity enables precise etching.

**Self-bias** — DC voltage that develops automatically in RF plasma due to electron/ion mass difference.

**Sheath** — Thin region near electrode where voltage drop is concentrated and ions are accelerated.

**Si-Ge**  (or **SiGe**) — Silicon-germanium alloy. Used as sacrificial layer under silicon nanosheet.

**Silane** — Silicon hydride (SiH₄). Precursor gas in some deposition processes.

**Silicon** — Element Si (atomic number 14). Substrate for semiconductor fabrication.

**SiO₂** — Silicon dioxide. Most common dielectric in semiconductor processing.

**Si₃N₄** — Silicon nitride. Used as hard mask and inter-layer dielectric.

**Small-scale feature** — In ARDE context, a narrow feature (small lateral extent).

**Sputtering** — Removal of atoms from surface by ion bombardment. Ion transfers momentum to surface atoms.

**Sputtering yield** — Number of atoms ejected per incident ion. Material-dependent.

**Selectivity ratio** — Same as *Selectivity*.

**Steady state** — Condition where plasma properties (density, temperature) remain constant over time.

---

## T

**Teardown** — Detailed engineering analysis of competitor's equipment.

**Temperature coefficient** — Rate at which etch rate changes with temperature. Negative (slower) or positive (faster).

**Ternary chemistry** — Etch gas mixture containing three components. E.g., Ar + CF₄ + C₄F₈.

**Threshold voltage** (or **Vth**) — Gate voltage at which transistor channel begins conducting.

**Throughput** — Number of wafers processed per unit time. Typically wafers/hour or wafers/day.

**Torr** — Unit of pressure. 1 Torr ≈ 1/760 atm ≈ 133 Pa.

**Transient** — Time-varying condition, as opposed to steady state.

**Trench** — Narrow, deep etched feature. Common in interconnect and isolation.

**TSMC** — Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company. World's largest foundry.

**Tungsten** (or **W**) — Metal used in interconnects and contacts. Often deposited in etched trenches.

---

## U

**Undercut** — Lateral etch beneath a hard mask or layer above. Can cause structural collapse.

**Uniformity** — See *Etch uniformity*.

**UV** — Ultraviolet light. High-energy photons used in lithography.

---

## V

**Vacuum** — Space with very few molecules. Etch chambers operate at partial vacuum (10–500 mTorr).

**Volatile product** — Chemical compound that readily evaporates/sublimates, leaving the surface.

**Voltage** — Electric potential difference, measured in volts.

---

## W

**Wafer** — Thin disk of silicon (typically 300 mm diameter, 0.725 mm thick) upon which circuits are fabricated.

**WFE** — Wafer Fab Equipment. Total value of semiconductor manufacturing equipment market.

**Wet etch** — Chemical etch using liquid (HF, KOH, etc.). Isotropic; rarely used in modern fabs.

---

## Y

**Y₂O₃** — Yttrium oxide. Fluorine-resistant coating used on chamber walls and electrodes.

---

## Z

**Zero-voltage switch** — Logic gate that switches when voltage passes through zero, reducing EMI.
